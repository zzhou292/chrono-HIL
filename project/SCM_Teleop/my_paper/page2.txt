                        Terramechanics-Aware Shared Control for
                      Off-Road Teleoperation on Deformable Terrain
                           Kyle Sha, Zhenhao Zhou, Radu Serban, Dan Negrut

                                   Department of Mechanical Engineering
                                     University of Wisconsin–Madison
                               1513 University Ave., Madison, WI 53706, USA

Abstract
We present a real-time, latency-aware human-in-the-loop framework for safe shared and autonomous
control of vehicles on deformable Soil Contact Model (SCM) [4] terrain. A differentiable neural sur-
rogate of the SCM tire force is embedded inside an ACADOS NMPC for terrain-aware trajectory gen-
eration, while a learned online estimator regresses the dominant soil parameters from vehicle propri-
oception so that both the planner and a latency-aware DOB-CBF-QP safety filter adapt to the ground
and to the round-trip delay in real time. The framework runs on a Chrono HMMWV instrumented with
Chrono::Sensor and driven by a Logitech G29 or keyboard fallback, with realistic teleoperation latency
sampled from learned 5G traffic models [2] injected over a ZMQ bridge.
Motivation and Contributions. Off-road teleoperation must contend with deformable-soil effects [1]
that rigid-terrain tire models cannot capture without per-deployment recalibration [3], while time-varying
network latency [2] requires latency-aware shared-control arbitration [8]. Building on Chrono [7] and
Chrono::HIL [9], we contribute: (i) a differentiable neural SCM tire surrogate that augments the static
MLP of [3] with rate-augmented and ResNet variants for an SQP-RTI NMPC; (ii) a learned online
terrain estimator that replaces the UKF of [3] and conditions both the surrogate and the safety constraints
from proprioception alone; (iii) a terrain- and latency-aware DOB-CBF-QP safety filter for obstacle
avoidance; and (iv) a massively parallel data and benchmarking infrastructure with a randomised unseen-
soil generator.

Proposed Framework
Real-Time HIL Stack. Chrono::Vehicle [5] runs an HMMWV on an SCM patch within Chrono::HIL [9].
Chrono::Sensor provides a driver-POV camera and a MEMS-grade accelerometer / gyroscope (Gaussian
noise + bias drift) that feed both the operator and the estimator. A second process running the NMPC and
CBF-QP is coupled to the simulator over ZMQ, which also injects time-varying teleoperation latency
sampled from learned 5G traffic models [2] rather than a constant offset (Fig. 1). The full stack closes
the loop in real time on a single workstation.

below 0.35 m and most below 0.10 m.


Differentiable SCM Surrogate. A small network maps wheel operating conditions and a reduced
Bekker / Mohr–Coulomb parameter set [6] to longitudinal and lateral tire forces. Where [3] employ
a static MLP, we additionally evaluate rate-augmented and ResNet variants that consume short propri-
oceptive histories (Fig. 1, right). All variants are trained on parallelised Chrono SCM tire-rig sweeps
and exported as CasADi expressions, so ACADOS auto-differentiates through the tire forces every solve
step.
Learned Online Terrain Estimator. Where Dallas et al. [3] drive an Unscented Kalman filter through
their NN tire model to identify the dominant terrain parameter, we train a window MLP that regresses
the Bekker sinkage exponent directly from a few seconds of proprioception (body velocities, yaw rate,
IMU accelerations, wheel speeds, steering, throttle), using dimensionless coupling channels that make
the descriptor approximately speed-invariant. The smoothed estimate re-conditions the surrogate and
the CBF traction bound online. Trained on a mixed open-loop / closed-loop corpus that spans both on-
manifold and off-manifold soil variations, the estimator converges within a few seconds on canonical
clay, dirt and sand (Fig. 2, left) and generalises to randomly sampled unseen soils with off-manifold
cohesion and friction perturbations. When this estimate re-conditions the differentiable surrogate, closed-
loop NMPC tracking on soft soils improves by an order of magnitude over Pacejka and TMeasy (Fig. 1,
right).
Terrain-Aware Trajectory Generation and Obstacle Avoidance. The autonomous loop is an NMPC
that minimises tracking, steering-rate and jerk penalties subject to a traction-aware lateral-acceleration
constraint and smooth softplus obstacle penalties, so the solver plans around obstacles within its hori-
zon. In HIL mode the operator’s delayed commands pass through a latency-aware DOB-CBF-QP safety
filter [8] with a heading-aligned ellipsoidal barrier elongated ahead of the vehicle, so steering around ob-
stacles is preferred over braking; safety margins and speed limits are computed online from the estimated
terrain and the margin is inflated by an EMA-estimated round-trip delay drawn from the same 5G traffic
model [2] that drives the latency injector.

Massively Parallel Data & Benchmarking Infrastructure. All training and benchmarking workloads
run on a process-level parallelism layer that orchestrates dozens of independent Chrono processes simul-
taneously, isolated by per-worker ZMQ ports and a shared YAML/CSV manifest. We open-source three
such pipelines: (i) a tire-rig sweep harness that fans out Latin-Hypercube SCM contact-patch simulations
across all available cores; (ii) a full-vehicle terrain-trace harness for many concurrent open/closed-loop
runs over procedurally generated on- and off-manifold soils; and (iii) a closed-loop benchmarking suite
that sweeps over (tire model, terrain, reference path, repeat) tuples in parallel – the 6 × 6 matrix in Fig. 1
(180 NMPC runs) is produced in under half an hour on one workstation. Models, scripts, terrains and
results are released publicly.
